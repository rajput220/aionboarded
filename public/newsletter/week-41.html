This Week in Artificial Intelligence

Anthropic 🕐 1 min read

Anthropic Files S-1 to Go Public, Revealing 14x Revenue Growth and Security Breach

Anthropic filed to go public on Nasdaq under ticker ANT, disclosing $16.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue — about 14 times the prior year. The filing also revealed an $80.7 billion accounting loss, $637 billion in compute supply agreements, and a security breach of internal business systems by startup Cathedral.

⚡ Why It Matters: This is the first time the public can see Anthropic's actual financial health rather than relying on leaks or company statements. The mandatory disclosures — including the breach and acquisition — show that IPO filings are becoming an unplanned transparency mechanism for the AI industry.
OpenAI 🕐 1 min read

OpenAI Claims Three Unverified Breakthroughs in Math, Computer Science, and Physics

OpenAI announced results in advanced mathematics, computer science, and physics this week — but acknowledges none have been independently verified. The physics claim, related to a Millennium Prize Problem, is being withheld until a Nobel laureate-led review is complete.

⚡ Why It Matters: This tests whether AI can genuinely extend human knowledge, not just summarize it. But "announced" is not "confirmed" — the gap between company claims and third-party verification is widening, which matters for anyone evaluating AI capability claims.
Industry 🕐 1 min read

Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon Raise AI Capex Guidance Past $700 Billion Combined

All three major cloud providers raised AI infrastructure spending guidance this week: Microsoft to $175 billion for fiscal 2027, Alphabet above $300 billion for 2027, and Amazon near $220 billion for 2026. The AI buildout is being financed with increasingly creative structures.

⚡ Why It Matters: The AI buildout is now large enough that it's being financed with increasingly creative and leveraged structures. Markets are starting to price in real doubt about whether all of it pays off.
Microsoft 🕐 1 min read

Microsoft Offers Voluntary Buyout to 60,000 Employees Alongside Record AI Spending

Microsoft offered a voluntary exit package to roughly 60,000 employees — about 27% of its global workforce — in the same week it posted record Azure growth and guided to $175 billion in AI infrastructure spending. No compensation terms or target departures were disclosed.

⚡ Why It Matters: Record AI spending guidance and a 60,000-person exit offer in the same week makes explicit what's usually discussed in the abstract: AI infrastructure investment and workforce restructuring decisions are moving together at the same company, in the same quarter.
OpenAI 🕐 1 min read

ChatGPT Adds Direct Access to iCloud Mail and Calendar

Apple users can now let ChatGPT read, search, and manage their iCloud email and calendar directly. The integration requires a dedicated app-specific password and only works with Apple email addresses, extending AI assistant reach into personal accounts.

⚡ Why It Matters: This is a significant expansion of what a general-purpose AI assistant can see and do. Reading and sending email, adding or changing calendar events — these are high-trust actions. The question is shifting from 'should AI have access' to 'how many permissions have users actually granted, and do they remember granting them.'
OpenAI 🕐 1 min read

GPT-6.1 Terra Launches for Visual Reasoning and Computer Use at New Price Point

OpenAI shipped a new speed-focused model combining visual reasoning with computer-use skills. Priced at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens, GPT-6.1 Terra more than doubled the prior generation's benchmark score on computer-operation tasks.

⚡ Why It Matters: A faster, cheaper model aimed at computer-use tasks suggests real progress on a capability that has lagged behind raw language ability industry-wide. The benchmark jump, while self-reported, indicates meaningful improvement in reliably operating computer interfaces.
Meta 🕐 1 min read

Meta Discloses $95 Billion AI Financing Plan Using Stock Sales and Preferred Equity

Meta disclosed a financing plan of up to $95 billion for AI data centers, structured through capital markets rather than cash reserves. NVIDIA, AMD, and Broadcom have expressed non-binding interest in up to $5 billion of the financing.

⚡ Why It Matters: This financing structure shows how AI infrastructure spending at scale is being funded with increasingly creative instruments. The chipmaker interest suggests suppliers are betting on their largest customers' continued buildouts.
Industry 🕐 1 min read

Amazon Posts Fastest AWS Growth in Five Years at 39%, Guides to $220B Capex

Amazon reported AWS revenue up 39% to $46 billion — its fastest growth rate in nearly five years — with a $578 billion services backlog. Despite strong numbers, the company guided to roughly $220 billion in 2026 capital spending.

⚡ Why It Matters: AWS's accelerating growth rate suggests enterprise AI workloads are driving cloud demand at a pace that surprises even optimistic forecasters. The massive capex guidance shows Amazon is betting this acceleration continues.
Google 🕐 1 min read

Alphabet's Google Cloud Revenue Doubles Year Over Year to $30.7 Billion

Alphabet reported Google Cloud revenue up 103% year over year to $30.7 billion, with total Q3 revenue of $127.6 billion. The company expects 2027 capital expenditures to exceed $300 billion and disclosed Gemini has reached 900 million users.

⚡ Why It Matters: A 103% cloud growth rate at Alphabet's scale is extraordinary — it suggests enterprise AI workloads are migrating to Google Cloud at a pace that outstrips even the broader cloud market's growth.
Security 🕐 1 min read

Security Startup Cathedral Breached Anthropic's Internal Systems in Under 12 Hours

Cathedral, a cybersecurity startup founded by former U.S. government DOGE staffers, says its AI agents accessed Anthropic's internal HR and sales systems in under 12 hours. The breach hit third-party vendor software, not Anthropic's AI models or customer data.

⚡ Why It Matters: Even a company whose entire business is AI safety can have ordinary security gaps in everyday business software. AI governance and conventional IT security are not substitutes for each other — both need attention.
Industry 🕐 1 min read

CoreWeave Credit Stress Persists as AI Infrastructure Debt Scrutiny Grows

CoreWeave, a major AI-cloud infrastructure provider, continued to show elevated credit-default-swap spreads this week — a market measure of investor concern about default risk. Spreads narrowed slightly but analysts say underlying credit stress persists.

⚡ Why It Matters: The AI buildout's reliance on debt financing is attracting market scrutiny. CoreWeave's credit spreads are a real-time indicator of investor confidence in whether AI infrastructure investments will generate returns to service the debt.
Research 🕐 1 min read

Moonshot AI's Kimi K3.1 Pro Available via API, Open Weights Coming November 4

China's Moonshot AI released its Kimi K3.1 Pro model via API with a 1-million-token context window, aimed at coding, agentic tasks, and financial research. Full open-weight files are due November 4, allowing organizations to self-host.

⚡ Why It Matters: This gives developers a chance to experiment with a leading Chinese open-weight model's capabilities via API now, ahead of being able to self-host when the weights release next week.

🔭 What to Watch Next Week

November 4 brings Moonshot AI's Kimi K3.1 Pro open-weight release — a 3-trillion-parameter model you can self-host. Watch for Anthropic's IPO roadshow pricing and the first independent reviews of OpenAI's math and physics claims. Microsoft's actual buyout participation numbers will reveal whether this is a trim or a transformation.
